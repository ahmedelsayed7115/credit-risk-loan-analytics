💡 Key Finding

Borrowers with credit scores in the 520–599 range have a default rate of 49.1% — nearly 4× higher than the 750+ group (11.7%). The jump between the 650–699 and 700–749 buckets represents a meaningful inflection point, dropping from 28% to 16.3%. The 700 threshold appears to be a natural risk boundary.

✅ Recommendation

Treat credit scores below 600 as high-risk requiring additional scrutiny or rejection. Scores 650–699 warrant stricter collateral or co-signer requirements. Borrowers with 700+ represent acceptable risk for standard loan approval.

💡 Key Finding — Years Employed

Borrowers with less than 2 years of employment have the highest default rate at 34.5% — nearly 2× the rate of those with 2–4 years (17.5%). This is the most significant employment-related risk signal. Interestingly, the 2–4 year group performs best, possibly reflecting younger borrowers with stable new jobs and manageable debt loads.

💡 Key Finding — Employment Status

Part-Time workers carry the highest default rate (27.7%), nearly 5 points above Contract workers (22.7%). Employment status differences are relatively modest compared to years employed, suggesting tenure is a stronger predictor than employment type.

✅ Recommendation

Flag applications from borrowers with less than 2 years of employment for additional review — their 34.5% default rate far exceeds the 24.3% average. Require proof of income stability or a co-signer. Part-time workers should face stricter DTI thresholds (recommend <40%).
